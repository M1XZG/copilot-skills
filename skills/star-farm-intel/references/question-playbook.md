# Question playbook

Use this as a progression, not a questionnaire. Ask one or two questions from
the earliest section that still contains a meaningful gap.

## Establish what they actually do

- What would you actually do with a repo like mine?
- Where would you show or share it?
- Who are the people you promote projects to?
- Is this something you do yourself or is there a team?

## Tie them to their examples

- What did you personally do for that repository?
- Can you show me what changed while you were working on it?
- When did you work with them?
- Is there someone on the project or a public post that confirms it?
- Do you have another example where your involvement is easier to see?

## Ask for measurable evidence

- Roughly how many stars or visitors did it have before and after?
- How do you tell which stars or visits came from your promotion?
- Do you provide a report showing where the attention came from?
- What happens if the promotion does not bring any interest?

## Learn about the audience and accounts

- Which sites or communities do you use?
- Do you have a GitHub profile I can look at?
- Are the people who star the repo expected to use it?
- How do you find developers interested in that type of project?
- Do you tell people that the promotion is paid?

## Understand the commercial offer

- What would it cost for a small repo?
- What exactly would I receive for that price?
- How long does it normally take?
- Is the price for promotion work or for a particular number of stars?
- Do you guarantee a number of stars, forks or visitors?

## Surface access and payment requirements

- Would you need access to my GitHub account or repository?
- Would I need to install or run anything?
- How do people normally pay you?
- Do you provide an invoice or written description of the service?

Any request for credentials, tokens, collaborator access, private code,
software installation or irreversible payment triggers the skill's stop
conditions.

## Follow-up patterns

When an answer is vague, ask for one concrete detail:

- I'm still not quite following. Can you give me a simple example?
- What did you do first on that project?
- Which part of the result came from your work?
- Is there anything public that shows you were involved?

When the sender avoids identifying themselves:

- What's your GitHub username?
- Do you have a page showing your previous work?
- How can I check that those projects used your service?

When they insist the engagement is organic:

- What does organic mean in practice here?
- Are people asked to star the repo, or do they decide after seeing it?
- Where do those people find the link?

Keep the wording natural and adapt it to the latest message. Do not copy several
questions into one reply.
